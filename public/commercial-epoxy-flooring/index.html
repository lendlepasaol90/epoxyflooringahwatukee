Commercial epoxy flooring for businesses that cannot close for long

A commercial floor has two jobs: hold up to daily traffic and cleaning, and stay out of the way of your business. We plan systems, schedules and phasing around both.

For a business owner, the floor is easy to ignore until it becomes a problem. Grout lines in the kitchen that never look clean. Vinyl tiles curling at the seams by the entrance. A bare concrete back room that sheds dust onto inventory. Then comes the hardest part: the thought of closing for a week to fix it.

Resin flooring solves most of those problems in one surface, and with the right system and planning, it does not have to shut you down. We work with retailers, restaurants, salons, medical and dental offices, fitness studios and offices around Ahwatukee and the nearby East Valley. Every commercial job starts with your schedule, then the floor.

Why businesses choose resin floors

Seamless and sanitary. No grout, no seams, no gaps for grime and bacteria. Coved edges can carry the floor up the wall a few inches so mops reach every corner.

Built for cleaning chemicals. Topcoats can be selected to resist the specific disinfectants, degreasers and chemicals your business uses daily.

Slip resistance where you need it. Texture can be adjusted by zone, with grippier finishes at entries, prep areas and wash stations and smoother finishes elsewhere.

Lower long-term upkeep. No waxing, stripping or grout sealing. Routine cleaning plus periodic topcoat renewal in high-traffic lanes.

A floor that matches your brand. Colors, sheens and decorative options let the floor support the look of the space rather than fight it.

Scheduling around your business

Downtime is the biggest cost of most commercial floor projects, often more than the floor itself. We plan to keep it small.

After-hours and weekend work. Many smaller spaces can be prepped and coated across an evening and a weekend using fast-curing systems.

Phasing. Larger spaces are split into sections. We coat one area while the rest stays open, then swap.

Fast-cure materials. Polyaspartic and other rapid systems allow foot traffic in hours instead of days when the space needs to reopen quickly.

Dust and odor control. Vacuum-attached grinders, containment barriers and low-odor products keep neighboring suites and shared HVAC systems happy.

You will get a written schedule before work starts, including when each area will be out of service and when it can be used again.

How a commercial project runs

  1. Site walk

    We review the space, current floor, moisture, traffic patterns, cleaning products and any landlord or code requirements.

  2. Proposal and schedule

    A written proposal with system specs, phasing plan and a calendar you can share with your team.

  3. Preparation

    Removal of old flooring, diamond grinding and repairs, with containment in occupied buildings.

  4. Coating

    System layers applied by zone, including coves, transitions and drains.

  5. Handover

    Final inspection, cure times, cleaning instructions and recommended maintenance intervals.

Many are leased suites with shared walls, shared HVAC and landlords who want to know exactly what is happening and when.

We are used to working within those limits: coordinating with property managers, keeping dust and odor contained, staging equipment so we do not block shared entrances, and leaving the suite ready for business when we say we will. For businesses in Tempe, Chandler and south Phoenix, the same approach applies.

Summer adds one more factor. Many commercial spaces have their HVAC set back after hours, and a hot suite changes how coatings behave. We confirm temperature plans with you ahead of time.

Why commercial floors need a specialist crew

Commercial floors raise the stakes. A failed coating does not just look bad; it can mean another closure, a safety hazard or problems with an inspection. The details that prevent that include moisture testing before coating over slabs, choosing the right texture for each zone, correct treatment of floor drains and transitions, and topcoats matched to your actual cleaning chemicals.

We take time on those details up front so the floor quietly does its job for years.

What affects the price of a commercial floor

Commercial floors are priced individually because the variables differ so much between spaces. The most important ones are the existing flooring to be removed, the condition and moisture level of the slab, the system chosen, details such as cove bases and floor drains, and the schedule. Night and weekend work, or tightly phased projects, take more planning and crew time than an empty suite.

We itemize these in your proposal. If budget is tight, we will show you where a lighter system makes sense, such as back offices, and where it is worth spending more, such as kitchens, entries and wet areas.

Looking after the floor after we leave

Most commercial resin floors need only routine cleaning with the right products. We give your staff written guidance on cleaners that suit the topcoat, how to handle spills and what to avoid, such as harsh solvents or abrasive pads on gloss finishes. Walk-off mats at entrances catch most of the grit that dulls floors over time.

High-traffic paths, such as the route from the kitchen line to the dish station or the main retail aisle, will show wear first. A maintenance topcoat in those areas every few years keeps the whole floor looking even and is completed in a fraction of the time of the original install.

Commercial flooring questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most before they book.

Can you work while we are closed overnight?

Often, yes. Many smaller spaces can be completed over a few evenings and a weekend using fast-curing systems. Larger spaces are usually phased so you can stay partially open.

Will the floor meet health inspection requirements?

Resin floors are commonly used in food service because they are smooth, durable and easy to clean. We build systems with coved edges and suitable textures, and we recommend confirming specific requirements with your local inspector during planning.

Can you coat over our existing VCT or tile?

We generally remove existing flooring and coat the concrete beneath. Coating over vinyl or tile risks bond failures and telegraphing seams.

How do we maintain a commercial resin floor?

Daily cleaning with an appropriate neutral cleaner, quick spill cleanup and walk-off mats at entries. High-traffic lanes may need a topcoat refresh after several years, which is far easier than replacing a floor.

Do you work with landlords and property managers?

Yes. We can provide product data sheets, safety data sheets, schedules and containment plans for building approval.

Can different areas of our space have different finishes?

Yes. It is common to use a textured, heavy-duty system in the kitchen or wash areas and a smoother decorative finish in the customer-facing space, with clean transitions between them.

How long does a commercial floor last?

Service life depends on traffic, cleaning practices and the system chosen. A well-installed resin floor with sensible maintenance typically lasts many years, and its working life can be extended by recoating worn lanes before the underlying layers are exposed. We give you a maintenance plan with every commercial floor.

Do you handle floor drains and transitions?

Yes. Drains, door thresholds and transitions to carpet or tile are where commercial floors most often fail. We slope, seal and detail these areas carefully, and in kitchens we usually carry the coating into the drain bodies so water cannot get under the edge.
