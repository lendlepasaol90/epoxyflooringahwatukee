Residential epoxy flooring for the rooms you live in

Coated concrete is cool underfoot, quick to clean and hard to damage, which is why more Ahwatukee homeowners are bringing it indoors. Here is how it works inside a home, and where it makes the most sense.

For a long time, "epoxy" meant garages and warehouses. That has changed. Designers have been specifying polished and coated concrete in desert homes for years, and the coatings themselves have caught up, with finer sheen control, better color stability and decorative options that look deliberate rather than industrial.

Inside a house, the goals shift. A garage floor is judged on toughness first. An interior floor also has to look right next to your cabinets and wall color, feel comfortable to walk on barefoot, and photograph well in daylight. We approach interior work with that in mind: more attention to sheen, consistency and edges, and a system chosen for the way the room is used.

Rooms where an epoxy floor earns its place

Interior coatings shine in rooms that see water, sweat, pets, tools or heavy traffic. These are the spaces we coat most often in Ahwatukee homes.

Casitas and guest suitesMany Foothills homes have a detached or attached casita on a slab. A satin coating keeps it cool and low-maintenance between visitors.
Home gymsSweat, dropped weights and rolling equipment. A thicker build with a textured topcoat handles all three.
Laundry and mudroomsWater, detergent and dirt from the garage door. A seamless floor has no grout lines to stain.
Game and media roomsMetallic or solid finishes give these rooms a finished, modern look with area rugs where you want softness.
Pet rooms and dog runsAccidents wipe up and claws do not scratch through a quality topcoat the way they do with wood or vinyl.
Open living areasWhere old carpet or cracked tile is coming out anyway, the slab underneath can become the finished floor.

Epoxy compared with tile and luxury vinyl

People weighing an interior coating are usually also pricing porcelain tile or luxury vinyl plank. Each has a place. Here is how they compare in a typical Arizona home.

Coated concrete is not the answer for every room. If you want the warmth of wood in a bedroom, vinyl or real wood may suit you better. But for high-use rooms, a coated slab is hard to beat on cleanability and durability, and it avoids the grout maintenance that comes with tile.

Finishes for interior floors

Interior finishes are chosen as much for mood as for performance. These are the options we discuss most.

Satin solid color

A low-sheen finish in warm greys, sand or soft white. Hides footprints and dust better than gloss, and looks calm in a busy room.

Metallic epoxy

Hand-worked pigments that move and shimmer. Makes a game room or living area the focal point. Read more on our metallic epoxy page.

Fine flake

Small chips in a tight blend read almost like terrazzo from standing height. Good for mudrooms and laundry rooms that take abuse.

Clear-coated concrete

If your slab has character worth keeping, a clear system can seal it while letting the natural grey show. Works best on slabs in good condition.

Preparing an interior slab

Interior prep starts with removal. Carpet, pad and tack strip come up easily. Tile and thinset take more work; we grind thinset down to clean concrete so no ridges telegraph through the coating. Glue from old vinyl or carpet has to be removed fully, because coatings will not bond to adhesive residue.

Then comes the part people do not see: moisture testing. Slabs-on-grade in older homes sometimes lack a good vapor barrier. Moisture coming up through the slab can push an interior coating off over time. We test, and if readings are high we use a moisture-mitigating primer before the decorative layers.

Indoors we also manage dust tightly, with vacuum-attached grinders, plastic barriers at doorways and HVAC returns covered.

Benefits you will notice living with it

Cooler rooms. Concrete holds onto the cool of the night. A coated slab feels pleasant underfoot through the long summer, especially in casitas and rooms that get afternoon sun.

Cleaner air. With no carpet fibers or grout lines, there are fewer places for dust, pet dander and pollen to collect. Monsoon dust that blows in is easy to dust-mop away.

Durability around kids and pets. Toys, scooters, claws and spills do not trouble a properly coated floor.

Design flexibility. The floor can be a neutral background or the main feature of the room.

Long service life. Interior floors see far less abuse than garages. With basic care, a quality interior coating can look good for many years, and the surface can be renewed with a fresh topcoat rather than replaced.

Interior project timeline

  1. Visit and samples

    We look at the existing floor, test moisture and bring finish samples to view in your light.

  2. Removal and grinding

    Old flooring and adhesive come up, the slab is ground and vacuumed, doorways sealed off.

  3. Repairs and leveling

    Cracks and low spots are filled; transitions to other floors are planned so thresholds sit cleanly.

  4. Primer and color coats

    Moisture-mitigating primer if needed, then the color, flake or metallic layers.

  5. Topcoat and cure

    A clear protective topcoat in your chosen sheen. Light foot traffic usually the next day, furniture after a few days.

Interior floors in a desert home

Arizona homes have their own quirks. Big sliding doors and windows let in strong sun, so in rooms with west or south glass we choose topcoats that hold their color. Baseboards in stucco homes are sometimes set right onto the old tile; we plan whether to remove and reset them or work up to them neatly.

Many homes in Ahwatukee were built with tile throughout the main living areas. When that tile is cracking or the style has dated, the cost to remove it and coat the slab is often competitive with retiling, with no grout to maintain afterward. If you are weighing that choice, we are happy to price both paths honestly, even if the answer is that tile makes more sense for your room.

Why interior coatings need a careful installer

Inside a home, small mistakes are very visible. Uneven sheen, roller marks, ridges from old thinset, dust specks in the topcoat and sloppy edges at baseboards all catch the light. Interior work also means protecting cabinets, walls and furniture, controlling dust in a lived-in home and planning transitions to other flooring so doors still close. We take the extra time interior projects need, and we will not start until moisture readings and prep say the floor is ready.

Interior epoxy questions

Straight answers to what people ask us most before they book.

Is an epoxy floor too hard or cold for living spaces?

It feels similar to tile. In our climate most people see the coolness as a plus. Area rugs add softness where you sit or stand for long periods, such as in front of a sofa or a kitchen counter.

Will it look like a garage floor inside my house?

Not if the finish is chosen for the room. Satin solid colors, metallics and fine flake blends look intentional and residential. Gloss levels and colors are very different from what goes into a garage.

Can you coat over existing tile?

We do not recommend it. Grout lines tend to show through and the bond depends on the tile glaze. Removing the tile and coating the concrete gives a better, longer-lasting floor.

How long will the room be out of use?

Most interior rooms take one to three days of work depending on removal, with light foot traffic the day after the topcoat and furniture back in a few days later.

Does the coating smell?

Some products have an odor during application. We use low-odor options where possible indoors, ventilate the space and explain any timing considerations if people are living in the home during the work.

Can epoxy go on a second floor or over wood?

Our residential systems are designed for concrete slabs. Wood subfloors move too much for these coatings. Most Ahwatukee homes are slab-on-grade on the ground floor, which is where interior coatings work best.

Does an interior coating add value to a home?

It is hard to put a number on it, but buyers tend to notice finished, low-maintenance floors, especially in casitas, gyms and laundry rooms. The bigger value is usually daily: easier cleaning and a surface that holds up to kids, pets and Arizona dust.
